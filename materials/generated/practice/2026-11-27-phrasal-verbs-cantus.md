# Social life · Common phrasal verbs & a reading about a Belgian student night

*Vocabulary, Reading · my level (A2+/B1) · about 45 min · 44 scored items + writing*

## 1. Learn it
**Read first (5–10 min):** [Perfect English Grammar · Phrasal verbs list 1](https://www.perfect-english-grammar.com/phrasal-verbs-1.html) and [Perfect English Grammar · Phrasal verbs list 2](https://www.perfect-english-grammar.com/phrasal-verbs-2.html). These pages show the **most common phrasal verbs in English** (the PHaVE list by Garnier and Schmitt). Each verb has one meaning and three examples. The eight verbs below come from these lists. The meanings follow the pages. The examples are about student life.

### What is a phrasal verb? (quick reminder)
A phrasal verb is a **verb + a small word** (*out, up, back, with*). Together they have a **new meaning**. *Find* + *out* = **get information**. You can't always guess the meaning, so learn each one with an example.

### Eight phrasal verbs for your social life
| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **go out** | go to a party, a bar or a restaurant | *Let's go out for dinner on Friday.* |
| **pick up** | go and get someone or something from a place | *My flatmate picked me up at the station.* |
| **come up with** | think of an idea or a plan | *Julie came up with a great idea for the party.* |
| **find out** | get information | *Can you find out what time the bar opens?* |
| **end up** | finally do or be something, often not planned | *We ended up going home early because it was raining.* |
| **turn out** | in the end, we discover something | *The quiet new student turned out to be really funny.* |
| **get back** | arrive home or at a place again | *I got back to Kortrijk at midnight.* |
| **figure out** | think until you understand (American English) | *I can't figure out these rules.* |

### Four grammar notes (from the tutor)
1. **Pronouns go in the middle** with *pick up*: ✓ *Can you pick **me** up?* ✗ *Can you pick up me?* With a noun, both are fine: *pick up Marta* / *pick Marta up*.
2. **end up + -ing**: ✓ *We ended up **taking** a taxi.* ✗ *We ended up to take a taxi.*
3. **turn out + to be** or **it turned out (that)**: *The party turned out **to be** great.* / ***It turned out that** the bar was closed.*
4. **get back to + place**, but **get back home** (no *to*), like *go home*: ✓ *I got back home at 2.* ✗ *I got back to home.*

**Typical mistake:** ✗ *Yesterday I didn't found out.* → ✓ *Yesterday I didn't **find** out.* After *didn't*, the verb is in the base form. The small word never changes.

## 2. Practice · Vocabulary
### A · Read the meaning, write the phrasal verb (in the right form)
1. Do you want to ___ on Friday? There's a party at the student bar. (= go to a party, bar or restaurant)
2. My train arrives at 6. Can you ___ at the station? (= come and get me, for example in your car)
3. We need a plan for Sara's birthday. Can you ___ something fun? (= think of an idea)
4. I don't know when the event starts. I'll ask Jens and ___. (= get the information)
5. We planned to stay for one drink, but we ___ staying until 2 a.m. (= finally did it, not planned)
6. I thought my new flatmate was shy, but he ___ to be really funny. (= in the end, we discovered)
7. The party finished at 1, and I ___ home at 1:30. (= arrived home again)
8. This board game is really hard. I can't ___ the rules. (= understand after thinking)

### B · Choose the small word
9. Last night I went ___ with my classmates, and we tried Belgian fries. a) on b) up c) out d) off
10. Julie came up ___ a great plan for the weekend. a) to b) with c) for d) at
11. Did you find ___ who is coming to the dinner? a) up b) on c) over d) out
12. We got ___ to Kortrijk very late after the trip to Ghent. a) up b) back c) out d) on
13. Don't take the bus. I'll pick you ___ at eight. a) on b) up c) out d) in
14. The film turned ___ to be really good. a) up b) on c) out d) off

### C · Choose the right form
15. It was raining, so we ended up ___ pizza at home. a) to eat b) eating c) eat d) ate
16. My bus arrives at 7. Can you ___? a) pick up me b) pick me up c) me pick up d) pick up to me
17. After the party, we got back ___ at 2 a.m. a) to home b) at home c) home d) in home
18. The "small bar" turned out ___ a big club with 300 people. a) be b) being c) to be d) that

### E · Rewrite the sentence with a phrasal verb (same meaning)
25. "In the end, we stayed at the party until midnight." → We ___ at the party until midnight.
26. "Later I discovered that the party was on Saturday." → It ___ that the party was on Saturday.
27. "Marta had a great idea for the costume." → Marta ___ a great idea for the costume.
28. "I returned to my flat at 1 a.m." → I ___ to my flat at 1 a.m.

## 3. Practice · Reading
### F · Read the blog post (read it twice, then answer)

> **My first cantus** · *by Lina, Erasmus student at Howest*
>
> Last Thursday, my Belgian classmate Jens asked me, "Do you want to come to a cantus?" I said yes, but I had no idea what it was. A cantus, I learned, is a student evening with songs. Everyone sits at long tables and sings from a little songbook called a *codex*.
>
> Jens said it started at eight, but that was wrong: the doors opened at eight, and the singing started an hour later. So we went for fries first. Belgian fries really are the best!
>
> A cantus has a lot of rules. One person, the "senior," leads the evening. You can't stand up or talk without permission. If you break a rule, you sing a song alone in front of everybody. After twenty minutes, I asked my friend Marta a question, and I ended up singing a Spanish song on a chair! Everyone clapped and sang with me.
>
> Most people drink beer, but you don't have to. I drank water and two sodas, and nobody cared.
>
> At first, I couldn't figure out the songs, because most of them are in Dutch or Latin. After an hour, I could sing the easy parts. Around one o'clock, Jens's flatmate picked us up in his car, and I got back home at half past one. I was tired, but happy.
>
> My advice for new Erasmus students: say yes when people invite you. You make friends much faster. The next cantus is on December 10, and Marta is already writing a funny song for it.
