# Pronunciation priorities for Portuguese speakers (American English model)

**Priority logic:** work on what hurts intelligibility first (Jenkins 2002 Lingua Franca Core; Munro & Derwing 1999), not on what merely sounds foreign. The order below runs from most to least important. Pick one issue per drill.

| # | Issue | Why it matters | Minimal pairs / drill | Real-life sentence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vowel length & quality /ɪ/ vs /iː/** | Changes the word: *live/leave, sit/seat, ship/sheep, it/eat, fill/feel* | ship–sheep, live–leave, full–fool (/ʊ/–/uː/) | *I live near the station. I leave at eight.* |
| 2 | **Final consonants with no added vowel** (epenthesis) | "Facebooki", "worki", "bigi" add syllables, which hurts word recognition | work, big, bed, laptop, stop, club; a /s/ cluster at the start: *school, study, Spain* (not "eschool") | *I work at a club. I study in Spain.* |
| 3 | **-ed endings** /t/ /d/ /ɪd/ | Adding /ɪd/ everywhere ("work-ed") sounds wrong and confuses tense | worked /t/, played /d/, wanted /ɪd/ | *I watched a movie and then I wanted to sleep.* |
| 4 | **Word stress** | Wrong stress makes known words unrecognisable | DEvelop → deVELop · COMputer → comPUter · hoTEL, PHOtograph / phoTOgraphy, Algorithm (AL-go-rith-um), DAta | *We developed a computer vision model.* |
| 5 | **TH /θ ð/** | In the Lingua Franca Core, /f/ or /t/ substitutes are tolerable, but /s/ can confuse ("I sink so" for *I think so*) | think–sink, three–tree, they–day, thank–tank | *I think there are three of them.* |
| 6 | **Initial H vs R** (for Brazilian Portuguese speakers: R as /h/) | *red* pronounced like "hed", *house* vs *rouse* | red–head, right–height, road–hold | *The red house on the right.* |
| 7 | **/æ/ vs /ɛ/ vs /ʌ/** | *bad/bed, man/men, cat/cut* | bad–bed–bud, cap–cup | *That man has a bad cat.* |
| 8 | **Final -m/-n nasalisation** | *time* ending in a nasal vowel ("tãi") instead of a closed /m/ | time, them, some, plan, ten | *See you then, same time?* |
| 9 | **Weak forms & schwa /ə/** | Mostly a *listening* issue: learners don't recognise *to, for, of, and, can* when they are reduced | *I can (/kən/) go. A cup of (/əv/) tea. Fish and (/ən/) chips.* | *I have to go to class.* |
| 10 | **American flap T** (water, better, city, a lot of) | Recognising it matters more than producing it | water, better, city, "what are you" → "whaddaya" | listen only |

## Drill format (10 min)
1. **Hear:** 2–3 YouGlish US examples of the target word: `https://youglish.com/pronounce/<word>/english/us`
2. **Contrast:** minimal pairs, slow then normal speed (the learner says one and you guess which; you can't hear, so use the dictation test: if the dictation writes the right word, it's a pass).
3. **Words:** 6 high-frequency words with the sound.
4. **Phrase:** 3 Erasmus phrases.
5. **Transfer:** use the phrase in a 1-minute mini role-play.
6. **Log:** record the issue and progress in `progress/error-log.md` (category: pronunciation).

**Dictation test:** the learner says a minimal-pair word into system dictation. If the transcript shows the intended word 4 times out of 5, mark it as intelligible. This is cheap, imperfect, but objective feedback.
