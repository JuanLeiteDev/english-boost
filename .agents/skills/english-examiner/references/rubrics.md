# Rubrics (paraphrased from the Cambridge English B1 Preliminary / B2 First assessment scales and the CEFR)

The official scales are in `materials/official/b1-preliminary/168150-b1-preliminary-teachers-handbook.pdf` (Writing Assessment, Speaking Assessment) and `materials/official/b2-first/167791-b2-first-handbook.pdf`. Read the PDFs when you need the exact wording. What follows is a working paraphrase.

Bands run 0–5. Bands 2 and 4 share features of the bands on either side.

## Writing: 4 criteria

| Band | Content | Communicative achievement | Organisation | Language |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5 (B1)** | Everything is relevant; the reader is fully informed | Follows the conventions of the task type (email, story, article) and holds the reader's attention; straightforward ideas come across | Generally well organised and coherent, with a variety of linking words and cohesive devices | A range of everyday vocabulary used appropriately, with occasional misuse of less common words; simple and some complex grammar with good control; errors don't block communication |
| **3 (B1)** | Minor irrelevances or omissions; the reader is informed on the whole | Uses the task conventions in generally appropriate ways | Connected and coherent, with basic linking words and a few cohesive devices | Everyday vocabulary, sometimes overused; simple grammar with good control; errors are noticeable but meaning is clear |
| **1 (B1)** | Irrelevance or misreading of the task; the reader is minimally informed | Simple ideas in simple ways | Connected with basic, high-frequency linkers (and, but, because) | Basic vocabulary used reasonably; simple grammar with some control; errors may sometimes block meaning |
| **0** | Content totally irrelevant | Below band 1 | Below band 1 | Below band 1 |

For B2-level judgments (C2 and C3 stretch tasks), a B2 band 3 roughly means: it communicates straightforward *and some complex* ideas, is well organised with a variety of cohesive devices, uses a range of vocabulary including less common words, and shows a range of simple and complex grammar with good control.

## Speaking: 4 criteria (at the B1 level, plus Global)

| Band | Grammar & vocabulary | Discourse management | Pronunciation | Interactive communication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | Good control of simple forms, attempts some complex ones; enough vocabulary to give and exchange views on familiar topics | Extended stretches despite some hesitation; relevant contributions; a range of cohesive devices | Intelligible; intonation generally appropriate; sentence and word stress generally accurate; individual sounds generally clear | Starts and responds appropriately; keeps the interaction going and develops it towards an outcome with very little support |
| **3** | Good control of simple forms; enough vocabulary for familiar topics | Responses longer than short phrases despite hesitation; mostly relevant, with some repetition; basic cohesive devices | Mostly intelligible, with some control of stress and intonation | Starts and responds appropriately; keeps the interaction going with very little prompting |
| **1** | Enough control of simple forms; limited vocabulary on familiar topics | Short phrases with frequent hesitation; repetition or digression | Mostly intelligible despite limited control | Keeps simple exchanges going with some difficulty; needs prompting and support |

**Transcript-based caveat:** pronunciation can only be estimated from patterns of dictation misrecognition and from the learner's self-reports. Mark it "(evidência limitada)".

**Fluency indicators** (supporting evidence, not a criterion): words per minute in a monologue, roughly ≥ 100 for confident B1+ and 70–100 for B1, with < 70 suggesting A2-level fluency on that task; the number of restarts; whether the learner can keep talking for 1–2 minutes.

## From scores to CEFR (internal heuristic)

- **Official objective parts** (Cambridge sample papers): ≥ 70% on a B1 Preliminary part is evidence of B1 for that skill; ≥ 60% on a B2 First part is evidence of emerging B2. On A2 Key parts, 85% or more suggests the learner is above A2. These are internal heuristics, not Cambridge Scale conversions. For those, see `materials/official/…/210434-converting-practice-test-scores…pdf` if downloaded.
- **Writing and speaking:** B1 bands averaging ≥ 3 across criteria = B1; ≥ 4.5 = B1+ (try a B2 task next time); < 2 = A2.
- **Grammar:** use the grammar-spine coverage. The learner is "at" a level when most units at that level are ✅/☑️ and stay so in the weekly mixed quizzes.
- **Vocabulary:** estimate from SRS data (`vocab.json`), the share of the B1 Preliminary vocabulary list known, and, optionally, the Updated Vocabulary Levels Test (Webb, Sasao & Ballance 2017).
- Always report a range when the sources disagree.
